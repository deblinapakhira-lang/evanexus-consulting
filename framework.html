The EvaNexus Framework

Four principles behind
every convening
I design.

This is the thinking that shapes every agenda, every discussion method, and every synthesis report I produce, regardless of sector or tier of engagement.

01

Research-Informed Practice

Agenda design too often relies on gut feeling and standard icebreakers. Structured group decision-making is a science. Evidence-based discussion methods help groups navigate ambiguity, weigh trade-offs, and reach genuine clarity — without defaulting to the loudest voice in the room.

02

Surface Real Tensions Early

Surfacing consensus is easy; surfacing the tensions underneath it is where real breakthroughs happen. High-stakes convenings should be designed to safely expose misaligned incentives, structural barriers, and competing priorities. Design space for healthy tension, and you unlock real alignment, not surface-level agreement.

03

Build Around One Central Question

A convening chasing five strategic questions at once will answer none of them well. Every session, breakout, and discussion protocol should anchor back to the one core question the gathering is explicitly tasked to resolve.

04

Design for the Day After

A convening that ends when the room empties hasn't done its job. Every agenda should be designed backward from the decision or artifact that needs to exist the day after — not just built forward from a good agenda.
